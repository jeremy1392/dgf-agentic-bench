# DGF FTE protocol (proposed)

Status: proposed field-research framework, outside the current LLM capability experiments. No enterprise cohort has been enrolled and no preregistration identifier exists. As of 24 September 2026, the baseline year has closed: testing the original 2033 prediction now requires an auditable retrospective reconstruction. A newly registered baseline and seven-year follow-up would be a separate hypothesis, not a postponed test of this one. No human-comparison study or recruitment is planned in the current experimental programme.

## Primary dated hypothesis

By 20 September 2033, the DGF ecosystem requires at least 80% fewer workload-equivalent full-time equivalents (FTE) than in 2026, at comparable governed output, quality, and service. The remaining ratio is at most 0.20. The paper's illustrative 140-FTE baseline gives a threshold of 28 FTE, including support.

FTE measures work, not individual people. Convert total human hours L into FTE using the fixed useful-hours convention K: N = L/K. The paper uses K = 120 useful hours per month. An empirical study must prespecify a consistent convention. Allocated staffing FTE and individual headcount are reported separately; neither replaces the primary workload measure.

## Calendar and boundary

- Baseline: average monthly required FTE over the twelve months ending on 20 September 2026. Final period: the corresponding year ending on 20 September 2033. Missing historical baseline records prevent confirmation of this dated test.
- Register organizations, activities, suppliers, sampling, weighting, and continuity rules before evaluation. Ecosystem-wide inference requires representative coverage; a convenience cohort tests its own population.
- Include internal effort, contractors, external reviewers, supplier operators, supervision, maintenance, recovery, rework, and all new support for the registered DGF functions. Record active hours in disjoint activity codes. Count shared support once before allocating it across customers.
- Count part-time contributions fractionally under the declared FTE convention. Outsourcing, renaming a team, or reallocating a support charge does not eliminate the underlying human work.
- Record mergers, exits, lost coverage, and scope changes. Unobserved supplier effort is missing, not zero. The illustrative 140-FTE enterprise pool is not an ecosystem census.

## Comparable-output measurement

- Record raw total FTE and FTE standardized to baseline project volume and case mix. Prespecify the matching or weighting method. Keep fixed support separate; do not scale it away when project volume changes.
- Include new support needed to execute the registered functions. Report new DGF activities and scope changes separately with sensitivity analysis; do not silently remove growing workloads.
- A decline caused solely by fewer projects does not confirm the primary hypothesis. Require preserved quality, service, and valid authority under prespecified margins.
- Distinguish the threshold result from AI attribution, which requires a comparative design addressing demand, restructuring, and other changes.

## Annual report

- Actual and standardized human hours and workload-equivalent FTE; allocated staffing FTE and individual headcount as separate outcomes.
- Exception labor, standard-path review, rework, and recurring support; total support hours separately from customer allocations.
- Project volume, case mix, quality, service, authority, missing data, and uncertainty.
- Residual human-intervention categories, hours, closures, new categories, and reopenings. Moving or renaming a category does not close it.

## Decision rules

- **FTE threshold:** the comparable-output final/baseline ratio must be at most 0.20 with quality, service, and authority conditions met. Above 0.20 rejects the prediction for the covered population. Report prespecified uncertainty intervals, organizational clustering, shared-supplier dependence, and time-measurement uncertainty. An interval spanning 0.20 is inconclusive; incomplete baseline or supplier effort prevents confirmation.
- **Illustration:** the configurations 140, 60.56, 16.35, 5, and 0 FTE retain their original assumptions. They have no assigned dates. Five support FTE leave at most 23 FTE for other work under the illustrative 28-FTE ceiling.
- **Complete closure:** any necessary human execution or support work prevents the corresponding zero-work endpoint. This stronger conjecture has no fixed deadline and is not required for the 80% FTE reduction.
- **Trajectory warning:** a residual workload ratio above 5% declining less than 0.5 percentage point per year for three years challenges continued decline, not automatically the 80% threshold. Residual exhaustion (H6) remains a separate test.
- Do not rescue a failed hypothesis by changing the FTE conversion, excluding supplier or support effort, dropping difficult sites, lowering quality, or postponing 2033. A staffing cut alone does not prove lower required work.

## Qualification

Contracts retain registered evidence, decision, quality, service, authority, and human-work requirements. Report qualification volume and dependence. Sixty failure-free visits only bound error below about 4.9% at one-sided 95% confidence, not 0.1%. The paper's 76-month work-package qualification window does not derive the FTE reduction.
