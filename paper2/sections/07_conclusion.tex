\section{Conclusion}

DGF-Bench shows that the tested models can complete many specified governance reviews under
explicit rules and accessible structured facts. Gemini attains 94.98\% strict gate success and
230 completely conforming routes among 299 evaluable projects. Its dispositions are correct
throughout that evaluable sample; its strict failures concern evidence conformity. Luna and
DeepSeek also show substantial task capability, with lower strict success and observed
substantive errors. The released traces permit these outcomes to be inspected instead of
reducing them to a single demonstration or a single accuracy number.

The executed rules control achieves complete strict success on all 300 dossiers without a
language model. This strengthens the evidence that the scaffolded review process can be automated,
while removing any inference that a language model is needed for this result. The incremental
value of models for unstructured evidence or ambiguous policies is not established here. If comparable performance can be achieved on operational evidence with
valid authority and acceptable assurance costs, automation could replace work currently
performed by employees and reduce the staffing required for DGF activities. That is the
task-substitution hypothesis advanced in this paper. An FDE contributes by making the policy,
evidence, mandates, handoffs, recovery, and human-work measurement function as one operating
system. The recurring work required to maintain it belongs in the result.

The deterministic control and structural evidence audit are now complete. The next decisive
evidence is a comparison with qualified humans, independently adjudicated support, scaffold-removal
tests, repeated model trajectories, and measured human hours in deployment. The proposed studies are designed to
separate a formatting repair from better judgment, task automation from merely parallel review,
and a staffing cut from lower required work. The new prospective 80\% FTE hypothesis uses a seven-year horizon from the end of a
registered baseline; no cohort has yet been enrolled. It cannot retrospectively confirm the
companion manuscript's original 2033 prediction. Complete governance automation
is not established as a law. The substantive claim is ambitious and testable: reliable execution
of review contracts may substantially reduce the human work required to govern projects.
