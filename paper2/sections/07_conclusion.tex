\section{Conclusion}

DGF-Bench measures rule application and evidence conformity under an explicit, favorable
information condition. The models often make correct decisions, but a deterministic program
solves the entire supplied contract. These results establish no incremental LLM advantage over
that control. Comparing systems that must first extract facts or formalize policy is the next
question, with the associated engineering and human costs included.

The all-model audit changes the interpretation of strict failures. Gemini's scores are highly
sensitive to excerpt serialization; the same relaxation removes substantially fewer DeepSeek
and Luna failures. Complete confusion matrices, procurement diagnostics, and both initial and
effective-reference agreement make the remaining errors inspectable. The 135-run follow-up
preserves the ranking while showing that similar aggregate rates do not imply stable success
for every dossier. A Word-only counterexample demonstrates why an extraction ablation must
first preserve enough information to determine the required answer.

These findings motivate an evidence interface that separates source attribution, premise
coverage, rule application, and authorized decisions. They support testing how much review work
such systems can perform in practice. They do not measure human work saved or a staffing effect;
those hypotheses require operational observations under independent quality assessment.
