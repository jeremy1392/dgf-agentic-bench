\subsection{Human work and the broader research hypothesis}
\label{sec:labor}

Review automation could replace work currently performed by people if it reduces total human
hours at comparable output, quality, and service. This requires measuring preparation, ordinary
review, exceptions, correction, and recurring support, including suppliers. Initial integration
and policy formalization must also be recorded for an investment comparison. Staffing,
workload-equivalent full-time equivalents (FTE), and individual headcount are distinct outcomes.
This benchmark measures none of them.

The companion manuscript develops a labor-accounting framework and an 80\% FTE-reduction
hypothesis for 2026--2033. That percentage is an author-selected research hypothesis, not an
estimate supported by these scores. The separate prospective protocol uses seven years after
a registered baseline ends; no cohort is enrolled, and that protocol cannot retrospectively
confirm the original dated claim. These field questions motivate the project but are not
empirical contributions of this benchmark study.

Task substitution and aggregate employment are distinct outcomes \citep{acemoglu2019}.
Residual oversight and maintenance matter even when routine execution succeeds \citep{bainbridge1983}.
Field productivity evidence such as \citet{brynjolfsson2025} concerns observed work in an
organization; its effect cannot be transferred to these synthetic project reviews.
