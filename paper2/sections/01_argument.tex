\section{Introduction}

Enterprise project reviews transform evidence into a decision, findings, and required actions.
A legal review checks contractual prerequisites; architecture and security reviews examine
technical choices and protections. Software can implement a review when its inputs, policy,
authority, and output contract are explicit. The central capability question is whether LLM agents can execute the decision tasks normally
assigned to human gate reviewers: inspect a dossier, identify applicable findings, select a
disposition, justify it with observed evidence, and respect authority. The study evaluates these
tasks under a declared contract; it does not compare model accuracy with measured human accuracy.

A Digital Governance Framework (DGF) organizes these review checkpoints.
DGF-Bench evaluates three tool-using model endpoints on connected synthetic governance reviews.
The information condition is deliberately favorable: agents can inspect executable policies
and authoritative structured facts as well as generated documents. This separates execution
of a specified contract from discovery of tacit rules or extraction of every premise from prose.
A deterministic comparator succeeds on the entire measured task. Consequently, the contribution
is an inspectable measurement of rule application, evidence conformity, and repeatability under
that condition. The control establishes task sufficiency; the model results measure execution
of the same decision contracts. No advantage over direct rule execution is established in this
information condition.

We report 899 original model--project runs on 300 projects, a 300-project deterministic control,
an exhaustive structural audit of the 690 gates with failed evidence components across all three
models, complete decision confusion matrices, and procurement diagnostics. Another 135 runs
repeat 15 existing dossiers three times per model. An offline document counterexample establishes
an information-sufficiency requirement for subsequent extraction experiments. Primary scores,
post-hoc sensitivity measures, repeated-run outcomes, and unexecuted protocols remain distinct.

\subsection{Related work}

Rule and policy application are established evaluation objects. RuLES tests adherence to
explicit conversational rules with programmatic evaluation \citep{mu2023}; LegalBench
assembles expert-designed legal reasoning tasks \citep{guha2023}. Business-process compliance
monitoring predates language-model agents and provides a basis for conventional rule-based
comparators \citep{ly2015}. DGF-Bench adds connected project-review outputs and evidence
contracts, but its synthetic rules are not an independently validated professional standard.

Evidence attribution is a separate issue from a correct answer. ALCE evaluates text generation
with citations \citep{gao2023}; the AIS framework specifies attribution to identified sources
\citep{rashkin2023}. Our lexical checker is narrower: it checks required observed references
and excerpts. Structural matching removes some serialization sensitivity but does not establish
full entailment or premise coverage. This distinction explains why a high decision score can
coexist with a substantially lower strict route score.

WorkArena evaluates enterprise software interactions \citep{drouin2024}; ITBench covers
operational IT scenarios across SRE, compliance/security, and FinOps \citep{itbench2025}.
$\tau$-bench evaluates policy-guided tool/user interactions, final database state, and repeated
reliability \citep{taubench2024}. CI-Work addresses contextual integrity in enterprise information
flows \citep{ciwork2026}, while AgentDojo evaluates adversarial tool-use settings
\citep{debenedetti2024}. DGF-Bench scores proposed reviews rather than completed operational
remediation; it has no live user conversation or adversarial robustness condition. Their
headline percentages are therefore not comparable. The importance of simple baselines, costs,
and held-out evaluation follows the analysis of \citet{kapoor2024}.

Agentic business process management raises broader questions of process-level integration,
monitoring, and escalation \citep{agenticbpm2026}. DGF-Bench supplies a bounded review-workflow
measurement within that agenda. Its released traces support studying where a model's contribution
might lie before adding it to an otherwise sufficient rule engine.

\subsection{Research boundary and companion manuscript}

This empirical study shares its original September 2026 experiment with the same author's
\emph{The Last Human Gate: Forward Deployed Engineering and the Automation of Enterprise
Governance}. The control, saved-trace audit, and repeated trajectories supplement that experiment;
they are not an independent replication on new projects. The companion retains the broader
FDE argument, labor accounting, and workforce scenarios. Here those implications are a short
discussion, not the principal empirical claim.

The workflows are author-designed and informed by practitioner experience. Their definitions,
artifacts, public rules, and scoring interface are released. Inspectability does not establish
that this policy represents any particular enterprise. The generator and evaluator share
rule definitions, so their agreement is internal consistency rather than independent validation.
