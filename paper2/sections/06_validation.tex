\section{A feasible validation program}
\label{sec:validation}

The empirical gaps require additional observations. The following studies are proposed; no
human participants, enterprise cohort, or independent semantic adjudication have been collected
for this paper. Their protocols should be finalized, independently reviewed, and registered
before new outcome collection. Existing benchmark inspections and design choices must be
disclosed as prior information. The companion files in \nolinkurl{paper2/protocols/} provide
an operational starting point rather than a claim of preregistration.

\subsection{Human and deterministic baselines}

A fair baseline must first specify which ability is being compared. A human with only Word
documents and a model with authoritative structured facts are not solving the same information
problem. The first comparison should therefore give qualified reviewers and agents the same
policy, accessible evidence, mandate information, output requirements, and case population.
Humans can use a form that captures structured outputs; record form-entry time separately
from investigation and deliberation. The exact-quotation requirement should be explained to
both groups, while a separate semantic evaluation tests support independently of serialization.

Evaluate at least three conditions: unaided human review, agent review, and human review of an
agent-produced draft. Add a deterministic policy engine that reads only the permitted facts and
mandates, without access to evaluator-only answers. This comparator tests whether the explicit
rule system itself accounts for disposition performance. Keep extraction from unstructured
documents as a separate experimental condition. An oracle that reads the answer key can check
the harness, but cannot serve as a substantive baseline.

Recruit domain-qualified participants, record relevant experience, and train them on development
cases excluded from evaluation. Assign held-out cases with counterbalanced order so that a
participant does not review the same case once unaided and again with an agent draft. Preserve
route-level context when measuring complete routes. Independently adjudicate answer quality
without revealing the review condition where practical. Report compensation, missing sessions,
exclusions, and any identifiable information retained under the study's ethics requirements.

Primary outcomes should include independently adjudicated disposition quality, false approval
and critical-miss rates, semantic evidence support, and active human minutes. Total cycle time
and exact-format conformity are secondary outcomes. For the assisted arm, count time verifying
the draft, finding errors, rewriting outputs, and escalating issues; a short initial reading
time alone is not an efficiency gain. Record time spent by other specialists who receive
questions from the assigned reviewer.

The number of cases and reviewers should follow a prospective power and precision analysis
using a stated noninferiority margin, expected paired disagreements, and participant/case
clustering. Three hundred synthetic cases do not by themselves determine the sample size needed
to establish rare-error safety or workforce effects. A small pilot may estimate variance and
operational burden, but its outcomes must not be used repeatedly to choose a favorable primary
metric. A confirmatory set should remain untouched until the analysis is frozen.

\subsection{Independent cases, scaffold removal, and repeated trials}

The generator and evaluator share an underlying rule system. An independent set should be
written or adjudicated by specialists who did not construct those rules, using policies and
documents whose ambiguities are documented. Specialists should be allowed to conclude that
a case is underdetermined. Such cases test escalation rather than forcing a single convenient
answer. Keep both policy disagreements and unambiguous implementation errors visible.

On a held-out synthetic set, compare full structured facts with a document-only condition,
then introduce stale evidence, conflicting versions, and incomplete records using frozen
transformations. Validate that the necessary facts remain recoverable when the task is intended
to be solvable. A drop after hiding \texttt{REVIEW\_FACTS} would quantify dependence on the
scaffold; it would not by itself distinguish extraction difficulty from reasoning difficulty.
Add an extraction-only measurement so that these components can be examined separately.

Repeat trajectories for every selected model--case pair using a predeclared repeat count,
reporting all attempts rather than selecting the best. Record provider, timestamps, resolved
model identifiers, inference settings, resource limits, and retries. Separate sampling over
projects from variability over repeated executions. If costs constrain replication, use a
stratified case sample whose selection is fixed before outcomes and retain its inclusion
probabilities. Expand the endpoint set to test whether the observed ranking survives a broader
model selection; the current three endpoints are not a population sample of AI systems.

Adversarial document content and prompt injection should form a separately declared condition.
Such tests concern whether the reviewer maintains its rules and authority while reading
untrusted evidence, as distinct from applying a trusted snapshot correctly. A favorable score
on the current dataset provides no estimate of that failure rate.

\subsection{Enterprise deployment: quality and labor together}

Field evaluation should begin with shadow review: the system produces outputs while existing
authority remains unchanged. Independently compare the outputs and measure the human work needed
to correct them. Shadow operation establishes review performance and integration burden; it
does not by itself demonstrate savings because it may add a parallel review. A later controlled
stage must measure actual substitution of defined activities under the approved operating design.

Where feasible, randomize comparable workflows or teams to staged adoption and retain a
contemporaneous comparison group. If randomization is not feasible, register a design using
pre-adoption trends, case-mix adjustment, and explicit confounders such as policy changes,
reorganization, demand changes, and other automation. A before--after reduction alone establishes
a temporal change, not attribution to the agent. Report both the volume-standardized effect
and the actual workload trajectory.

The activity ledger should include evidence preparation, ordinary review, exception handling,
remediation coordination, quality checks, incident work, and recurring system support. Shared
platform teams and supplier operators must be counted once, then allocated under a frozen rule.
Unobserved external effort is missing, not zero. Maintain an obligation register so that apparent
savings cannot be achieved by leaving corrective work unfinished or pushing it beyond the
measurement window. The observation period must include scheduled maintenance and policy updates.

Quality requirements should be registered with domain owners before deployment: admissible
decision error, critical misses, unauthorized actions, evidence validity, and service delay.
Noninferiority on an average score is insufficient if it hides unacceptable critical failures.
The primary labor reduction is interpretable only while those requirements are met. If a
site reverts to manual review, its recovery and manual effort remain part of the outcome.

\subsection{Repairing the 2026--2033 calendar}
\label{sec:calendar}

The original dated hypothesis is that required DGF FTE will be at most 20\% of the 2026
baseline by 20 September 2033, at comparable governed output, quality, and service, including
recurring support. Its baseline is the twelve-month period ending 20 September 2026; its
endpoint is the twelve-month period ending 20 September 2033. As of this manuscript, the
baseline window has already closed and no cohort is enrolled. It cannot be opened prospectively
after the fact.

There are two legitimate paths. The first is a historical-baseline study: enroll organizations
with auditable records covering the original window, reconstruct the activity boundary, validate
time attribution, and freeze the reconstruction before examining endpoint outcomes. Register
record eligibility and missingness rules before selecting sites. Include an audit of contracts,
timesheets, case volumes, supplier effort, and support allocation. Retrospective estimates and
their uncertainty must be disclosed; missing history cannot be replaced by recollection without
changing the evidential status of the comparison.

The second is a prospective cohort with a new baseline. Fix a registration date $t_0$, measure
twelve months of baseline activity, and specify a follow-up period before observing its outcomes.
It can test subsequent labor reduction, but it is a new dated hypothesis. A seven-year comparison
from a later baseline has a later endpoint. A comparison ending in 2033 instead has a shorter
interval. Neither should be reported as confirmation of the original 2026--2033 prediction.

For the original test, estimate a volume- and case-mix-standardized final/baseline labor ratio
including support. Register organizational weighting, clustering, attrition treatment, and
uncertainty from time reconstruction. Confirmation requires the upper bound of the registered
uncertainty interval to be at most 0.20 and all quality and service criteria to pass. A lower
bound above 0.20 contradicts the threshold for that population. An interval spanning the
threshold is inconclusive; an estimate below it with missing material labor is not confirmation.
Failure to enroll an eligible cohort leaves the forecast untested rather than moving its date.

A convenience cohort supports claims about its participating organizations. Ecosystem-wide
inference requires a defensible sampling frame, coverage of functions and suppliers, and
appropriate weighting. Employment consequences also require reporting staffing and headcount
separately. The causal claim that AI produced the change requires the comparison design in
addition to observing the threshold. These distinctions specify what data must be collected;
they do not weaken the possibility of a large reduction when those data support it.
