\section{Limitations and validation priorities}
\label{sec:validation}

\textbf{Task and external validity.} The generator, public policy, and evaluator share an
author-designed rule system. The comparator establishes sufficiency of this supplied system,
not independent business validity. Decision balancing improves coverage but changes prevalence.
There are 300 project fact sets and 267 whole-case finding patterns; some gate-level facts recur.
The experiment does not measure discovery of tacit policy, real-world remediation, or human
judgment. The business validity of these author-designed rules is not established by the experiment.

\textbf{Evidence metric.} Exact excerpts test a serialization contract. The post-hoc
lexical-or-structural endpoint is intentionally more permissive and was motivated by observed
failures; it is not a new preregistered primary score or a semantic success rate. It retains
original lexical passes, including overly broad quotations. Neither test establishes complete
premise coverage. All-model diagnostics and unchanged primary scores are released so the effect
of the relaxation is inspectable. A semantic evaluation requires independent labels for failed
and passing evidence under a fixed rubric.

\textbf{Sampling and repeated execution.} The original study has one retained trajectory per
model--case pair. The follow-up has only 15 existing dossiers and three trajectories per model;
its cluster intervals condition on that sample. The three endpoints, including two Flash
variants, are not representative of all systems. Provider routing was not fixed, and infrastructure
resumes retain completed prefixes. No causal attribution of variability to intrinsic model
randomness is made. Novel held-out cases, matched information conditions, and broader endpoint
coverage are needed to test generalization.

\textbf{Costs and baselines.} API costs omit human formalization, document preparation, integration,
local compute, maintenance, and review. The deterministic program has no model output-token
limit and cites whole observed snapshots; its 100\% result establishes task sufficiency, not
an equal-resource optimization result. A qualified-human baseline and a fixed-budget comparison
of complete extraction-and-decision systems remain absent.

\textbf{Task capability and human comparison.} The study evaluates execution of governance
review tasks ordinarily assigned to people. There are no measured human decisions, participants,
or enterprise cohort, and an independent-human evaluation is outside the current programme.
Consequently, the results quantify model conformity to the contract, not parity with human
reviewers, human work saved, or employment effects. Automation bias remains relevant to eventual
human use of these outputs \citep{parasuraman2010}, but is not measured here.

The next paid studies should test policy representation, evidence extraction, and matched
General-gate handoffs under a versioned protocol. The document-only counterexamples and the 300-case source inventory demonstrate why
simply deleting supplied facts is insufficient. Runtime tools can also restore hidden snapshots. The original benchmark remains frozen; new
information and evidence contracts must be reported as a separate experiment.
