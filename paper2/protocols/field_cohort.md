# Proposed enterprise cohort and calendar

**Status: no enrolled organizations, no baseline audit, no preregistration identifier.**

## Primary prospective hypothesis

Register baseline start `t0` before collection, observe twelve months ending at `tB`, and fix the endpoint `tF = tB + 7 calendar years`. The primary hypothesis is that standardized required FTE in the twelve months ending `tF` is at most 20% of baseline, with quality and service preserved. Dates are assigned at actual registration, not invented in this manuscript. A hypothetical 1 October 2026 baseline start gives a 30 September 2027 baseline end and 30 September 2034 endpoint.

## Original dated hypothesis (historical context only)

At comparable governed output, quality, and service, required DGF workload-equivalent FTE in the twelve months ending 20 September 2033 is at most 20% of that in the twelve months ending 20 September 2026. Include internal effort, contractors, suppliers, oversight, rework, and recurring maintenance. Convert hours using one fixed FTE convention. Record staffing and headcount separately.

The original baseline window is closed. The study can test this hypothesis only for populations with a credible historical baseline. A 2026–2033 label is not justified merely because enrollment begins during 2026.

## Path A: historical baseline

Before examining endpoint outcomes, register eligible records, coverage requirements, sampling, reconstruction method, quality thresholds, missingness rules, and analysis. Audit timesheets, contracts, case volumes, supplier labor, and support allocation. Freeze the reconstructed baseline and document uncertainty and exclusions. Missing material history prevents confirmation; historical recall alone must not be presented as prospectively observed hours.

## Path B: new prospective baseline

Register a start date `t0`, collect a twelve-month baseline, and predeclare the follow-up interval. A later baseline with a seven-year horizon has a later endpoint; an endpoint in 2033 gives a shorter interval. State the resulting hypothesis explicitly. Do not rename it as the original test.

## Operating boundary and design

Begin with shadow operation to assess quality and integration, then use a controlled stage measuring actual task substitution. Prefer randomized/staged adoption when feasible; otherwise document comparison groups, pre-trends, case mix, restructuring, demand, and other automation. Shadow operation alone may add work and cannot establish savings.

Use disjoint activity codes for evidence preparation, review, exceptions, corrections, handoffs, incidents, and support. Allocate shared supplier effort once. Treat unobserved hours as missing. Record unfinished obligations and recovery after failed deployments. Report both actual and volume/case-mix-standardized work; do not scale away fixed support. Record initial build effort separately and include it in investment/payback assessment.

## Decision and generalization

Prespecify quality/service noninferiority and critical-error limits. With those conditions met, an upper uncertainty bound at or below 0.20 supports the original threshold for the covered population; a lower bound above 0.20 contradicts it. An interval spanning the threshold is inconclusive. Missing material labor or absent baseline prevents confirmation.

Register organizational weights, clustering, attrition, reconstruction uncertainty, and lost coverage. A convenience cohort cannot establish an ecosystem-wide estimate without an appropriate sampling frame. Observing the threshold and attributing it to AI are separate claims. No enrollment leaves the forecast untested; it does not justify postponing the original deadline silently.
